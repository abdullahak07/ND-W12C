01
CIA TRIAD

Protect the secret. Protect the truth. Keep it running.

The CIA triad is the core framework: confidentiality protects sensitive data, integrity protects accuracy, and availability keeps systems accessible when needed.

CConfidentiality

Only authorised people should see sensitive information.

Example: your banking password should not be visible to strangers.
IIntegrity

Information should remain correct and unaltered.

Example: nobody should secretly change your exam mark from 80 to 20.
AAvailability

Systems should be accessible when legitimate users need them.

Example: an emergency hospital system cannot simply be offline.

05
ETHICAL CHALLENGE · DATA PRIVACY

Just because we can collect data does not mean we should.

Ethical privacy problems include over-collection, missing consent, tracking and surveillance, and organisational misuse of personal information.

OVER-COLLECTIONCollect only what you genuinely need.A weather app does not need your full contact list.
CONSENTPeople should understand what they agree to.A buried 40-page policy is not meaningful explanation.
TRACKINGContinuous monitoring changes the ethical risk.Location histories can reveal home, work and routines.
MISUSEData collected for one purpose can be abused for another.“We collected it legally” does not justify every later use.

ETHICAL CHALLENGE · RESPONSIBILITY

When a system fails, “the computer did it” is not an answer.

The lecture asks who is responsible: developers, organisations, or both? Legal responsibility and ethical responsibility can differ, so transparency and accountability must be designed into systems.

DEVELOPERDid the implementation ignore known risks?
ORGANISATIONWere unsafe priorities, policies or deployment choices imposed?
SHAREDDid multiple decisions across the lifecycle contribute?
LAW vs ETHICSSomething can be legal and still be irresponsible.
